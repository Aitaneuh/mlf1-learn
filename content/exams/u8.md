Clustering, KNN and topic models. Pass mark: 70 %.

:::quiz
? Which statement is correct?
+ K-means is unsupervised (K = number of clusters); KNN is supervised (K = number of neighbours)
- Both are supervised
- K means the number of iterations in both
> Same letter, different algorithms.

? The two alternating steps of K-means are…
+ assign each point to its nearest centroid; recompute each centroid as the mean of its points
- vote among neighbours; store the data
- compute TF-IDF; factorise
> Until assignments stop changing.

? Points 2, 4 and 9 (1-D) are in one cluster. Where is the updated centroid?
= 5
> Mean of the members.

? Inertia keeps decreasing as K grows. Why is this not a reason to choose a large K?
+ More clusters always reduce inertia; with K = n it is 0, but the grouping is useless
- Inertia increases with K
- Inertia is a supervised metric
> Need other criteria (silhouette, usefulness).

? A silhouette score below 0 for a point suggests…
+ it may fit another cluster better than its own
- it is perfectly clustered
- it is the centroid
> Negative = closer on average to a neighbouring cluster.

? What does ARI = 1 mean?
+ The clustering partitions the data exactly like the labels (IDs may differ)
- 100 % accuracy for every cluster ID equal to the digit
- No agreement
> ARI ignores the arbitrary naming of clusters.

? Why should you not compare an ARI of 0.4 with a KNN accuracy of 0.95 as if KNN were “better by 0.55”?
+ They are different metrics measuring different things for different tasks
- Because ARI is always lower
> Report clustering and classification metrics in separate tables.

? KNN with K = 3, neighbours' labels [2, 2, 9]. Prediction?
= 2
> Majority vote.

? Which is true about KNN's computational cost?
+ fitting is cheap (store data), prediction is expensive (distances to all training points)
- fitting is expensive, prediction is cheap
> All the work happens at prediction time.

? Using the majority training digit of each cluster to name it and then measuring accuracy gives…
+ a label-assisted cluster classifier — no longer purely unsupervised
- the unsupervised accuracy of K-means
> The mapping uses labels (and must never use test labels).

? In TF-IDF, a word that appears in almost every document gets…
+ a low weight
- a high weight
> IDF down-weights common terms.

? NMF factorises the document–term table into W (documents × topics) and H (topics × terms). Which statement is true?
+ A document can have non-zero weight on several topics
- Each document belongs to exactly one topic
- W contains probabilities that sum to 1
> NMF weights are non-negative, not probabilities.

? Who gives names to the topics found by NMF?
+ We do, after reading top words and representative documents
- NMF outputs the topic names
> Topics are patterns, not meanings.
:::
