Application evaluation, usability, A/B testing and decisions. Pass mark: 70 %.

:::quiz
? App A: 97 % model accuracy, 82 % task completion, low satisfaction. App B: 93 % accuracy, 98 % completion, high satisfaction. Which statement is best?
+ B may be the better solution; model accuracy is only one level of evaluation
- A is better because its model is more accurate
> Users experience the whole application.

? “Uptime 99.9 %” is evaluated at which level?
+ application / technical
- user
- model
> Availability of the system.

? “Return on investment after two years” belongs to…
+ the business / organisational level
- the model level
> Financial value for the organisation.

? SUS responses: all odd items 4, all even items 2. Score?
= 75
> Odd: 5 × 3 = 15; even: 5 × 3 = 15; 30 × 2.5 = 75.

? A SUS score of 68 is…
+ roughly the commonly cited average
- 68 % of users satisfied
- a failing grade
> SUS is not a percentage.

? In a within-subjects A/B test, how do you reduce learning effects?
+ counterbalance the order of the applications
- give more help to the second group
- use different instructions per group
> Half start with A, half with B.

? Independent variable in an A/B test comparing two apps?
+ the application
- task completion time
- the SUS score
> What is deliberately varied.

? A between-subjects design typically needs ___ participants compared with within-subjects.
+ more
- fewer
> Each person contributes to only one condition.

? Participants recorded times like “5”, “7min”, “4-5 min”. Best practice?
+ Parse clear formats, flag approximate/unclear values explicitly, keep the raw value for auditing
- Delete all non-MM:SS rows silently
- Replace everything with the average
> Transparent cleaning decisions.

? “Completed” in the study means a diagram was submitted. Why is that not task success?
+ Success requires the result to meet predefined requirements, assessed e.g. with a rubric
- Because completion time is missing
> Completion ≠ correctness.

? Which survey question is neutral and focused?
+ “How difficult was Task A with this tool? (1 = very easy … 5 = very difficult)”
- “How much faster was tool A than tool B?”
- “Don't you agree tool A is easier?”
> No assumption, one aspect.

? Weighted scores: usability 0.6, efficiency 0.4. Tool X = (8, 6), tool Y = (6, 9). Score of Y?
= 7.2
> 0.6·6 + 0.4·9 = 7.2 (X: 7.2 as well — a tie! Priorities decide.)

? 12 first-semester students preferred tool B. Which conclusion is supported?
+ Most participants in this experiment preferred B
- Most BFH students prefer B
- B is objectively better for everybody
> Don't generalise beyond the sample and tasks.
